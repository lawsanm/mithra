# **1\. Executive Summary** {#1.-executive-summary}

Mithra — meaning *friend* in both Sinhala (මිත්‍ර) and Tamil (மித்ரா) — is a web-based community platform that helps neighbours in the same Grama Niladhari (GN) division lend, share, and give to each other. Members earn points by lending out items they already own; they spend those points when they need to borrow something themselves. Mithra is intentionally hyper-local: one GN division per deployment, a single trusted Moderator per division, and a closed-loop points economy funded entirely by sponsors.

Members never spend cash on the platform — by design. The closed-loop economy is funded by sponsor companies whose cash is divided at two levels: first into a points fund (which becomes community points at a 1:1 backing) and an operating overhead; then the points are allocated across six designated uses including general circulation, aid grants, moderator stipends, festival drops, and reserves. Sponsors can override the default allocation if they want their funds tied to a specific purpose.

Three principles shape every feature of Mithra:

* **Lend** — points-based borrowing of everyday items between verified neighbours.

* **Share** — peer gifting and the trust score that lets neighbours decide who to lend to and from.

* **Care** — permanent donations, aid grants, and disaster relief, all designed to keep the community supporting itself.

The platform is built as a server-rendered PHP application with a MySQL database and a vanilla HTML/CSS/JavaScript front-end — no frameworks, no libraries. This deliberate simplicity keeps the codebase accessible to a four-person team, the hosting requirements minimal (any standard LAMP environment), and the focus where it belongs: on the community logic, not on tooling.

# **2\. The Story Behind Mithra** {#2.-the-story-behind-mithra}

Pradeep lives in a Nugegoda apartment. Last year he bought a power drill for Rs. 8,500 to hang three picture frames. It has lived in his cupboard ever since. Two floors below, Nimali is putting up curtain rails this weekend and is about to spend another Rs. 8,500 on the same drill.

They live in the same building. They have nodded at each other in the lift. Neither of them knows the other has, or needs, that drill.

This story repeats itself across every neighbourhood in Sri Lanka, every day. Suitcases, projectors, sewing machines, baby cots, camping tents, large cooking pots, pressure washers, board games, and ladders — bought once, used twice, and stored forever, while neighbours quietly buy the same items or simply go without.

| The items already exist. The need already exists. What’s missing is a way to connect them. |
| :---- |

On Mithra, Pradeep lists his drill. Nimali, verified as a member of the same GN division, sees it and borrows it for the weekend. Pradeep earns 80 points he can later use to borrow her sewing machine. The drill comes back on Sunday. Both rate each other in the app. Nothing dramatic happens. They smile in the lift on Monday.

Multiply that across a building, a lane, a division, and the cupboards of an entire neighbourhood become a shared library of things.

## **2.1 Three modes, one platform** {#2.1-three-modes,-one-platform}

**Lend.** Everyday items, point-based, between verified neighbours. Pradeep and Nimali and the drill — the platform’s everyday case.

**Share.** Sometimes the borrower doesn’t have items to lend yet, or the lender wants to send their neighbour a small thank-you. Peer gifting lets members send small point gifts for any reason — birthdays, splitting a rental, helping a new member get started.

**Care.** A young family who has just moved in needs to borrow a fan but has nothing yet to lend. Their Moderator vouches; the Sponsor Liaison approves; an aid grant covers their borrowing — quietly, without anyone in the lane knowing it was a grant. Or when May rains flood half the lane, Disaster Mode alerts sponsors to the emergency and connects them directly with the moderator, who arranges relief on the ground.

# **3\. Problem Statement & Motivation** {#3.-problem-statement-&-motivation}

## **3.1 What we are solving** {#3.1-what-we-are-solving}

* Households accumulate items that sit idle 95% of their lifespan.

* Borrowing from neighbours informally is socially awkward; it rarely extends beyond the closest two or three households.

* There is no fair, traceable way to track who borrowed what, the agreed price, and the return condition.

* Cash-based rental platforms feel transactional, exclude lower-income participants, and discourage neighbourly help.

* Families in genuine need have nowhere to turn for one-off borrowing — charities focus on permanent aid, neighbours feel awkward, formal rental excludes them by cost.

* During disasters, donation flows happen on WhatsApp groups — fast but chaotic, with no record of who needs what or who has helped.

## **3.2 Why this fits as a one-year project** {#3.2-why-this-fits-as-a-one-year-project}

* Clear, bounded scope — one GN division per deployment.

* Substantial domain logic — point system, condition photos, validation, gifting, aid, disaster — well beyond standard CRUD work.

* Five distinct actors with genuinely different permission sets.

* Real-world testability — a pilot in one GN division is feasible.

* Strong social impact angle for evaluation.

## **4.4 How Mithra thinks about points** {#4.4-how-mithra-thinks-about-points}

Internally, sponsor cash funds the points pool at a 1:1 backing so that pricing decisions can be grounded in real item values. This backing is an implementation detail visible only to Admin and Sponsor Liaison in their accounting views.

Members see only points throughout the UI. A drill’s declared value, the rental rate, the penalty for damage — all expressed as point figures. No rupee conversion is shown to members anywhere. Points are presented and treated as platform credit, earned by participating in the community, not as a currency.

Because members never put cash into the platform and never withdraw points as cash, Mithra is structurally cleaner than a typical e-money or rental platform. The platform handles no member money. Cash flows in only from sponsors; cash never flows out.

# **5\. The Five Actors** {#5.-the-five-actors}

Borrower, Lender, Donor and Recipient are all the same Member role — every member can play any of these depending on the situation. There are four additional actors who support and govern the platform.

| Actor | Who they are | What they do |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Member** | A verified resident of one or two GN divisions (one permanent home community, one optional temporary community). | List items, browse, request, confirm handover and return, donate, gift, request aid, rate. Each member has one home community and may hold one active temporary community at a time. |
| **Moderator** | One trusted person per GN division, known personally to most residents. Holds a 500-point conduct bond. | Verify new members, approve listings and value proofs, vouch for aid grants, mediate contested damage cases in person and record the agreed outcome. |
| **Sponsor Liaison** | Platform-wide role. The link between sponsor companies and the platform. | Onboard sponsors, record sponsor purchases, inject points into pools, approve aid grants, generate CSR reports, coordinate Disaster Mode rollouts. |
| **Admin** | The platform operator (project team in production; lecturer/panel during evaluation). | Manage divisions, appoint moderators and liaisons, view global analytics, handle escalated disputes, toggle Disaster Mode, manage item categories. |
| **Sponsor** | A company funding the points pool for CSR visibility. | Purchase point packages, view CSR impact reports, upload branding, designate funds for specific purposes via the override mechanism. |

# **6\. Member Capabilities** {#6.-member-capabilities}

Mithra has a single member tier. Every verified member of a GN division has the same capabilities and the same rights. There are no subscriptions, no annual fees, no collateral deposits, and no cash transactions with the platform — by design.

## **6.1 What every member can do** {#6.1-what-every-member-can-do}

* List items for lending, with declared-value proof (Section 9).

* Browse and request to borrow items listed by other members in their GN division.

* Confirm handover and return of items, with photo records (Section 10).

* Donate items permanently to other members.

* Send small point gifts to other members in the same GN division, subject to caps (Section 11).

* Request an Aid Grant when in genuine need (Section 12).

* Rate and review other members after every transaction.

* Close their account at any time (Section 18).

* Join a temporary community in a new city while keeping their home community active (Section 6.4).

## **6.2 What members cannot do** {#6.2-what-members-cannot-do}

* Top up their wallet with cash.

* Pay a subscription, deposit collateral, or otherwise transact in real currency with the platform.

* Convert points back to cash.

If a member wants to borrow something but does not have enough points, the platform’s answer is to encourage them to list items they have at home for lending — earning the points they need through participation. Members in genuine hardship can request an Aid Grant.

## **6.3 Trust score and member reputation** {#6.3-trust-score-and-member-reputation}

Because there are no tier-based or value-based borrowing caps, members rely on each other’s reputation to decide whether to lend a valuable item. Every member has a single trust score — a value from 0 to 100 — shown on their profile and on every item listing.

### **6.3.1 The five weighted factors**

| Factor | Weight | How it is measured |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Average rating (R)** | 40% | Average star rating from completed transactions, scaled to 0–100. |
| **Completed transactions (V)** | 20% | Number of completed transactions, capped at 50\. |
| **Return reliability (L)** | 20% | On-time return rate, excluding donations. |
| **Member tenure (T)** | 10% | Length of verified membership, capped at 12 months. |
| **Community contribution (C)** | 10% | Based on donations made, capped at 100\. |

| Weighted score Computed \= 0.40R \+ 0.20V \+ 0.20L \+ 0.10T \+ 0.10C |
| :---- |

### **6.3.2 Confidence blend (Bayesian shrinkage)**

A member with a single five-star rating should not outrank a member with fifty consistently good transactions. To prevent new members from starting with an unfairly high or low score, the computed score is blended with a neutral prior of 50:

| Confidence blend S \= (K × 50 \+ n × Computed) ÷ (K \+ n) where K \= 10 and n \= the number of completed transactions. |
| :---- |

A new member therefore starts at exactly 50\. As completed transactions accumulate, the displayed score gradually converges on the member’s actual calculated score — reputation is earned through genuine participation rather than a lucky first rating.

### **6.3.3 Penalty deductions**

Penalties are applied after the confidence blend:

* −5 points for each upheld damage claim.

* −10 points for each past account suspension.

* −5 points for each shortfall-covered incident within the previous 12 months.

The final score is then clamped to the 0–100 range and rounded.

### **6.3.4 When the score updates**

* Automatically after every completed transaction.

* Through a nightly scheduled cron job (which also refreshes tenure).

### **6.3.5 How the score is used**

* Displayed on every member profile and item listing, alongside a division-specific context line (e.g. ★ 78 overall · 12 completed in Jaffna · 3 completed in Colombo).

* Lenders evaluate borrower reliability before approving a request.

* Moderators reference it during dispute resolution and other trust-related decisions.

* It forms part of the moderator eligibility criteria (Section 17).

* A low score does not block participation — it is informational, leaving each lending decision to the member.

# **7\. The Point System** {#7.-the-point-system}

## **7.1 The base unit** {#7.1-the-base-unit}

1 Point is the indivisible base unit. There are no fractions. Calculations round up to the nearest whole point in the system’s favour during penalties (favouring lender) and in the borrower’s favour during refunds.

## **7.2 How points enter the system** {#7.2-how-points-enter-the-system}

Points only enter the system from sponsor cash. There is no member cash inflow.

* **Sponsor purchase.** A company pays cash to the Sponsor Liaison. Points are credited at a 1:1 backing (subject to the two-level cash split in Section 15). The only inflow of new value.

* **Welcome bonus.** 200 points credited from the Sponsor Pool to each newly verified member.

* **Moderator stipend.** 100 points per month per active moderator, from the Sponsor Pool.

## **7.3 How points move within the system** {#7.3-how-points-move-within-the-system}

| Movement | From | To | Trigger |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Rental charge** | Borrower | In-Flight → Lender | Handover confirmed by both parties (Section 10). |
| **Late-fee buffer refund** | In-Flight | Borrower | On-time return, no damage. |
| **Moderator-decided outcome** | Configurable | Configurable | Moderator records the agreed resolution; both parties sign off. |
| **Late fee** | Borrower | Lender | Return is late (automatic). |
| **Gift** | Sender | Recipient | Member gifts points to another member (Section 11). |
| **Aid grant** | Aid Pool | Recipient | Liaison approves a vouched request. |
| **Donation** | (none) | (none) | Item ownership transfers; no points move. |

## **7.4 How points exit the system** {#7.4-how-points-exit-the-system}

| Closure type | When it applies | What happens to the balance |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Type A — Standard** | Voluntary closure by any member. The default. | Remaining points move to the Retired Pool and are recycled back to the Sponsor Pool on schedule. |
| **Type B — Donation (parting gift)** | Member leaving the area opts in to donate their balance. | Remaining points move to the GN division’s Aid Pool, tagged with the member’s name as a parting gift. |

Reserve Pool write-off: uncleared point debts older than 60 days may be written off by Admin; those points exit via the Retired Pool.

## **7.5 The six pools** {#7.5-the-six-pools}

Every point in the system at every moment lives in exactly one of six internal pools:

| Pool | Purpose |
| :---- | :---- |
| **Sponsor Pool** | Sponsor-funded points awaiting distribution as welcome bonuses, stipends, festival drops. |
| **Aid Pool** | Reserved for aid grants to members in genuine need. Replenished from 15% of every sponsor injection. |
| **Reserve Pool** | Active safety net. Covers shortfalls when borrowers cannot pay agreed penalties or late fees. No member ever carries a negative balance; Reserve absorbs the gap (logged as write-off). |
| **In-Flight Pool** | Holds rental charge and late-fee buffer during active rentals. |
| **Member Wallets** | Points held by individual members. The sum of every member’s balance. |
| **Retired Pool** | Closed accounts and written-off debts, awaiting recycling back to Sponsor Pool. |

| The Nightly Invariant Check Σ (Sponsor \+ Aid \+ Reserve \+ In-Flight \+ Wallets \+ Retired) \= Σ inflows − Σ outflows Every night, a PHP CLI script triggered by cron sums the six pools and verifies the total equals what was injected from sponsors minus what has exited. Any drift triggers a critical-bug alert. This is the platform’s most important integrity check. |
| :---- |

## **7.6 Late fees (automatic)** {#7.6-late-fees-(automatic)}

* Every rental holds a late-fee buffer of one daily rate in In-Flight.

* 1–24 hours late: buffer forfeited to lender.

* 25–72 hours late: buffer \+ 1 day’s rate per additional 24h block deducted from borrower’s balance.

* \>72 hours late: item flagged as unreturned. Moderator alerted. Treated as total loss after 7 days, handled via moderator-led resolution (Section 10.2).

## **7.7 Worked example** {#7.7-worked-example}

Pradeep’s drill — declared value around 8,500 points. Pradeep lists it and sets his own rate: 85 points per day.

* Nimali (balance 500 points) borrows for 2 days. At handover acceptance, 170 \+ 85 \= 255 points move from her wallet to the In-Flight Pool (rental charge \+ one-day late-fee buffer).

* At handover confirmation by both parties, 170 moves to Pradeep’s wallet. The 85-point buffer stays in In-Flight.

* On-time return, both parties confirm condition matches handover baseline: 85 buffer returns to Nimali. Pradeep finishes with \+170 points net.

* If Nimali drops the drill (a minor scratch): a damage claim is raised and the moderator decides the outcome (Section 10). All damage cases follow this single moderator path.

* If the casing is badly chipped (Moderate damage, contested): moderator path. All three meet in person. Moderator decides the outcome. All three sign off in the app.

## **7.8 No negative balances — Reserve Pool guarantee** {#7.8-no-negative-balances-—-reserve-pool-guarantee}

**Mithra has one absolute rule:** a member’s point balance can never go negative.

If a late fee, damage penalty, or other deduction would exceed a member’s available balance, the Reserve Pool covers the shortfall. The member incurs no debt; the community reserve absorbs the gap.

### **Why this rule exists**

* Prevents member debt servitude — no one is trapped working off points they owe.

* Simplifies moderation — moderators decide what is fair without calculating ability to pay.

* Sponsors are part of the solution — the Reserve Pool is sponsor-funded, so sponsors accept that community safety nets have a cost.

### **What happens when Reserve covers a shortfall**

* The shortfall is logged as a Reserve Pool write-off, permanent and traceable.

* At the nightly invariant check, the write-off reduces the Reserve Pool balance.

* If the Reserve runs low, the Admin notifies the Sponsor Liaison to top it up.

* The member’s account shows a zero or positive balance; there is no debt record.

# **8\. Rental Pricing** {#8.-rental-pricing}

Mithra does not impose fixed rental rates. Lenders set their own daily and monthly rates when listing an item. This keeps the platform simple, respects each lender’s judgement about their own item’s worth, and avoids the need for a centrally maintained rate schedule.

## **8.1 How a lender sets a rate** {#8.1-how-a-lender-sets-a-rate}

* Lender creates a listing and enters a daily rate and/or a monthly rate in points.

* The item’s validated declared value is shown on screen for context, so lenders can price sensibly relative to what the item is worth.

* Lenders can offer daily only, monthly only, or both. If both are offered, borrowers see whichever is cheaper for their requested duration.

* Lenders can update their rates at any time (changes do not affect active bookings).

## **8.2 Pricing guidance (non-binding)** {#8.2-pricing-guidance-(non-binding)}

The platform displays a non-binding guideline when the lender is setting a rate:

| Pricing Tip Community lending typically works well at 1–2% of declared value per day. For a 5,000-point item, that’s 50–100 points per day. You’re free to set any rate — this is just a starting point. |
| :---- |

This is a soft nudge, not a rule. No rate cap, no minimum, no moderator approval needed for pricing. The market — neighbours deciding whether a rate feels fair — is the regulator.

## **8.3 Categories** {#8.3-categories}

Items are still assigned to a category (Tools & Hardware, Household Appliances, Electronics, Books & Media, etc.) for browsing and search purposes. Categories affect how items appear in search results and filters — but they do not determine pricing.

## **8.4 What the borrower sees** {#8.4-what-the-borrower-sees}

* The listing shows the daily rate, the monthly rate (if offered), and the declared value.

* When requesting a specific duration, the system highlights which option (daily or monthly) is cheaper.

* Requests longer than 27 days are nudged toward the monthly rate if one is offered.

* The borrower’s trust score view of the lender, and the lender’s rating history, help borrowers judge whether the price is fair for the neighbourhood.

# **10\. Condition Photos & Damage Handling** {#10.-condition-photos-&-damage-handling}

Mithra protects both lenders and borrowers through two mechanisms: handover and return condition photos (which create an immutable record of the item’s state before and after the rental), and a moderator-led resolution process for all damage claims. All damage disputes are handled fairly in person with full transparency.

## **10.0 Condition baseline — handover and return photos** {#10.0-condition-baseline-—-handover-and-return-photos}

Before a borrower takes an item, and again when they return it, both parties record the item’s condition with photos and notes in the app. The handover record becomes the immutable baseline against which the return condition is compared.

### **10.0.1 At handover**

When lender and borrower meet at the agreed pickup time:

* Each party uploads 1–5 photos of the item from different angles. The platform stamps each photo with timestamp and uploader’s user ID using PHP’s GD library.

* Each party can add a free-text note describing any pre-existing scratches, dents, missing accessories, or condition details.

* The combined record is shown to both for review. Each party then clicks Accept or Cancel.

| Decision | What happens |
| :---- | :---- |
| **Both Accept** | Handover record is locked as the baseline. Rental charge moves from In-Flight to lender wallet. Borrower takes the item. Rental is officially in progress. |
| **Either party clicks Cancel** | Booking cancels cleanly with no penalty. In-Flight points return to borrower immediately. No late fees, no point movements. Both can rate each other on the cancellation. |
| **No decision within 48 hours** | Booking auto-cancels. Points return to borrower. Both parties notified. |

### **10.0.2 At return**

When the borrower returns the item, the same photo-and-notes process repeats. The platform displays the handover baseline alongside the return record for direct comparison.

| Decision | What happens |
| :---- | :---- |
| **Both Accept — return matches baseline** | No damage claim. Late-fee buffer returns to borrower (if on-time). Booking closes. Both parties rate each other. |
| **Lender raises damage claim, borrower contests or claim is moderate/major** | Moderator path. Moderator sees both handover and return photos side by side. |
| **Lender raises claim, borrower contests** | Moderator path. Moderator sees both handover and return photos side by side. |
| **No return record within 48 hours** | Item flagged as not yet returned. Late fees begin. |

## **10.1 Damage resolution** {#10.1-damage-resolution}

All damage claims are resolved through the moderator path (Section 10.2). The moderator sees both handover and return photos side by side, hears both parties, and decides a fair outcome.

## **10.2 Moderator resolution process (in-person)** {#10.2-moderator-resolution-process-(in-person)}

Used for all damage claims. Lender and borrower present their cases; moderator decides the outcome.

* Booking enters ‘pending moderator’ state. No automatic point movement.

* All three — lender, borrower, moderator — arrange to meet in person within 7 days.

* The platform shows the moderator the handover baseline and the return photos side by side, plus any evidence either party uploaded.

* Moderator hears both sides and decides the resolution.

* All three update the platform: moderator records the outcome; lender and borrower each click ‘I agree with this resolution’. Booking closes only when all three have signed off.

* If a party refuses to sign off, the case escalates to Admin within 7 days. Admin’s decision is final.

## **10.3 Damage severity reference** {#10.3-damage-severity-reference}

| Severity | Definition | Guideline penalty |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Minor** | Cosmetic; missing accessory \<10% of value. | 20% guideline |
| **Moderate** | Functional but reduced; missing accessory 10–40% of value. | 50% guideline |
| **Major** | Usable after repair. | 80% guideline |
| **Total loss** | Destroyed, lost, stolen, or unreturned \>7 days late. | 100% guideline |

# **11\. Peer Gifting** {#11.-peer-gifting}

Members can send small point gifts to other members in their GN division — for thanks, birthdays, helping a neighbour, splitting a rental.

## **11.1 Rules and guardrails** {#11.1-rules-and-guardrails}

* Available to all verified members.

* Recipient must be in the same GN division.

* Daily limit per sender: 200 points. Annual limit per sender: 2,000 points.

* Every gift requires a short reason (free text, max 100 characters).

* Gifts blocked while sender has any pending damage claim, unresolved dispute, or point debt.

* Gifts blocked while recipient has any pending damage claim or point debt.

* Gifts are irreversible once sent. No undo, no refund.

* Members can disable receiving gifts in settings.

## **11.2 What gifting is NOT for** {#11.2-what-gifting-is-not-for}

Paying off another member’s damage penalty or aid debt. Routing around any rule the platform enforces. Daily and annual limits enforce these by design. Patterns of suspicious gifting (e.g. round-trip transfers, large flows to a single recipient) are flagged to the moderator.

# **12\. Aid Grants** {#12.-aid-grants}

Aid is for members in genuine need who cannot afford to borrow what they require. It is vouched by the Moderator and approved by the Sponsor Liaison.

## **12.1 Principles** {#12.1-principles}

* **Vouched, not openly requested.** Moderator vouches with a comment before the request goes to the Liaison.

* **Two-step approval.** Moderator vouches → Sponsor Liaison approves.

* **Capped.** 500 points per member per year, one active grant at a time.

* **Purpose-tied.** Each grant is linked to a specific intended borrowing.

* **Audit-trailed.** Every grant — request, vouch, approval, use — recorded in the ledger permanently.

## **12.2 Workflow** {#12.2-workflow}

* Member fills ‘Request Aid Grant’: purpose, evidence (optional), requested amount.

* Moderator vouches with a comment or rejects respectfully within 5 days.

* Sponsor Liaison reviews vouched request and member’s aid history.

* Liaison approves (possibly adjusted), rejects with reason, or asks for more info.

* On approval, points move from Aid Pool to member wallet, tagged ‘Aid Grant — \[purpose\]’.

* Member uses points within 30 days. Half of unused returns to Aid Pool; annual cap adjusts accordingly.

## **12.3 Safeguards** {#12.3-safeguards}

* Cooling period: 60 days between grants.

* Moderators cannot vouch for their own family members — conflict-of-interest declaration required.

* Aid vouching excluded from moderator’s monthly activity count (no perverse incentive).

* Pattern of suspicious requests triggers Admin review.

# **13\. Donations** {#13.-donations}

Mithra supports permanent transfers of items in a single unified flow called Donations. Whether a member wants to give away something they no longer need or contribute to a disaster-affected neighbour, the mechanism is the same: a free, permanent ownership transfer with no points moving between parties.

## **13.1 How donations work** {#13.1-how-donations-work}

* Lister selects listing type ‘Donation’ instead of ‘Rental’. No rental rate is set.

* Moderator approves the listing. Fast-tracked during Disaster Mode.

* Interested recipients request the donation. The donor chooses one recipient (or sets first-come-first-served).

* Both parties meet for handover. Donor confirms transfer; recipient confirms receipt.

* On handover, ownership transfers. No points move. No return expected. No damage penalty.

* The donor receives a non-currency donor badge on their profile.

## **13.2 What is and is not donatable** {#13.2-what-is-and-is-not-donatable}

* Donatable: clothes, furniture, electronics, kitchenware, schoolbooks, toys, household items.

* Donatable with extra review: food (only during Disaster Mode, with freshness windows), unopened medicines within expiry (only during Disaster Mode).

* Not donatable: illegal items, weapons, live animals, expired food, opened medicines.

# **14\. Disaster Mode** {#14.-disaster-mode}

Admin can toggle Disaster Mode on a GN division during emergencies (floods, landslides, fires). It is an alert-and-connect bridge: it alerts sponsors to the emergency and connects them directly with the division’s moderator, who arrange relief between themselves off-platform. It does not change the points economy.

## **14.1 What Disaster Mode actually does** {#14.1-what-disaster-mode-actually-does}

Disaster Mode is deliberately simple: it is an alert-and-connect bridge between the community and its sponsors during an emergency. It does not change the points economy, adjust caps, or move any points. All relief is arranged directly between the sponsor and the moderator, outside the platform.

* **Moderator reports the disaster.** The moderator of the affected GN division notifies the Admin that an emergency (flood, landslide, fire, etc.) has occurred.

* **Admin activates Disaster Mode.** After verifying, the Admin toggles Disaster Mode on for that specific GN division.

* **All sponsors are alerted.** Every sponsor receives a notification that there is a disaster in the named GN division.

* **Platform connects the parties.** Using the platform network, an interested sponsor is connected directly with the division’s moderator. They coordinate relief between themselves.

* **Relief is arranged off-platform.** If the sponsor chooses to help, they provide cash or goods directly to the moderator. Mithra does not process this contribution and no points are involved — the points economy is never touched.

* **Sponsor Liaison verifies and records.** The Sponsor Liaison sits in the middle to verify and record the transferred amount, so the contribution can be documented in the sponsor’s CSR report. The Liaison does not gate or approve the relief — the role here is purely verification and record-keeping.

## **14.2 Turning it off** {#14.2-turning-it-off}

* Admin sets Disaster Mode with a planned end date and switches it off when the emergency has passed.

* Turning it off simply stops the alerts and the active-emergency status for that division. Because no in-system rules were changed, nothing needs to be reverted.

* The Sponsor Liaison’s verified records of any contributions remain available for the sponsor’s CSR reporting.

# **15\. Sponsor & CSR Model** {#15.-sponsor-&-csr-model}

Sponsor contributions are the only funds that enter Mithra. Members never pay the platform — sponsors do. Every rupee contributed becomes exactly one point, with no deductions, so 100% of a sponsor’s contribution reaches the community through the points economy.

## **15.1 How contributions are collected** {#15.1-how-contributions-are-collected}

* Sponsor contributions are collected offline by the Sponsor Liaison under a written agreement.

* Each contribution is recorded in the system with the contribution amount, receipt number, and the sponsor’s chosen allocation.

* The platform never processes the cash itself; it records only the resulting points.

## **15.2 The 1:1 point creation rule** {#15.2-the-1:1-point-creation-rule}

| 1 Rupee \= 1 Point — no deductions Every rupee contributed is converted into an equal number of points. Nothing is withheld, so 100% of every sponsor contribution reaches members through the points economy. |
| :---- |

## **15.3 Sponsor-directed allocation** {#15.3-sponsor-directed-allocation}

At contribution time, the sponsor decides how their points are split across two pools. The split is entirely the sponsor’s choice — any percentage combination is permitted, including 100% to a single pool.

| Pool | What the sponsor’s points fund |
| :---- | :---- |
| **Sponsor Pool (General)** | Welcome bonuses for new members, moderator stipends, and other approved community rewards. |
| **Aid Pool** | Reserved exclusively for approved aid grants to members in genuine need. |

**Example:** a sponsor contributing Rs. 100,000 who chooses a 70/30 split creates 70,000 points in the Sponsor Pool and 30,000 points in the Aid Pool. A sponsor who wants their entire contribution to help members in need can direct 100% to the Aid Pool.

Every allocation is permanently logged against the originating contribution, so each point in the system is traceable to a specific sponsor and their declared split.

## **15.4 Accountability and transparency** {#15.4-accountability-and-transparency}

* **Six dedicated pools.** All points are managed through the Sponsor, Aid, Reserve, Retired, In-Flight, and Member Wallet pools.

* **Append-only ledger.** Every point transaction is permanently recorded and never edited or deleted.

* **Nightly verification.** An automated process validates that the total points across all pools always equal the total points created, ensuring no points are lost or created without authorization.

* **Transparency dashboard.** Members and sponsors can view current pool balances, sponsor contributions, point allocations, aid distributions, and the result of the latest verification run.

* **Independent external audits.** Mithra maintains comprehensive financial records and undergoes periodic independent external financial audits by a qualified third-party auditing team, verifying that all contributions are properly recorded, managed according to approved budgets, and used solely for their intended community purposes.

## **15.5 What sponsors receive** {#15.5-what-sponsors-receive}

* Branded recognition — sponsor wall, monthly newsletter, and optional tags on welcome bonuses and aid grants funded by their contribution.

* A CSR impact dashboard showing items shared, households reached, aid grants enabled, and estimated community savings.

* A quarterly impact report generated by the Sponsor Liaison as a print-friendly page.

# **16\. Moderator & Sponsor Liaison Compensation** {#16.-moderator-&-sponsor-liaison-compensation}

## **16.1 Moderator stipend** {#16.1-moderator-stipend}

* 100 points per month per active moderator.

* Paid only with at least one moderation action that month. Aid vouching excluded to avoid perverse incentive.

* Inactive moderators flagged after 60 days; Admin replaces them.

## **16.2 Moderator conduct bond** {#16.2-moderator-conduct-bond}

Moderators hold a 500-point conduct bond as a minimum wallet balance, separate from their spendable balance. The bond exists because Moderators have system access and dispute authority.

* 500-point bond credited from the Sponsor Pool at appointment.

* Bond cannot be spent down. Penalties deducted from bond first, then spendable balance.

* Bond at zero: moderation privileges auto-suspended.

* Voluntary resignation in good standing: bond returns to Sponsor Pool.

* Removal for cause: bond forfeited to Reserve Pool; incident logged permanently.

## **16.3 Sponsor Liaison compensation** {#16.3-sponsor-liaison-compensation}

The Sponsor Liaison is compensated entirely from the 10% operating overhead portion of every sponsor purchase — in real money, not in points. They do not receive any platform points as stipend.

* In production: a part-time salary or contractor fee drawn from the overhead bucket.

* In the project demo: the project team plays the Liaison role at no cost; overhead is simply noted in accounting without distribution.

* **Why no points stipend:** The Liaison is a company-side role, already compensated by the overhead. Giving them points on top would be double-dipping from community funds.

# **17\. Moderator Trust & Misconduct Safeguards** {#17.-moderator-trust-&-misconduct-safeguards}

The Moderator is the very person who normally resolves disputes. When they themselves are a party to a transaction, the dispute model cannot rely on them. Mithra addresses this with four layered safeguards.

## **17.1 Moderator selection — three-phase bootstrap model** {#17.1-moderator-selection-—-three-phase-bootstrap-model}

Mithra uses a three-phase approach that solves the bootstrap problem (no members have tenure or trust history on day one) and transitions into data-driven selection as the platform matures.

### **Phase 1 — Admin-run (0 to \~10 members)**

At launch, no moderator exists. The Admin manages the division directly:

* The Admin approves new registrations by checking the evidence submitted during sign-up (NIC photo, proof of address) against the GN division register.

* Listings, aid requests, and any early disputes are handled by the Admin.

* This phase is intentionally short — at community scale, processing a handful of registrations is trivial for the Admin.

### **Phase 2 — Admin appoints the first moderator (at \~10 members)**

When the division reaches approximately 10 approved members, the Admin appoints the first moderator from the approved pool — deliberately, not by registration order and not by election.

**Why not the first member?** Being first to register means being the most enthusiastic, not the most trustworthy. In a role that verifies neighbours’ identities and resolves disputes, ‘whoever signed up first’ is a weak basis for appointment.

**Why not elections?** Elections optimise for popularity, not judgement. They create factions in a small community where everyone knows everyone, which is corrosive when the winner then adjudicates disputes between neighbours. A 10-member electorate is also too small to be meaningful, and the build cost (candidacy, voting, ballot integrity, tie-breaking) is disproportionate.

**How the Admin selects:** the Admin reviews the approved members and picks the candidate who best meets the bootstrap criteria:

| Bootstrap criterion | Requirement |
| :---- | :---- |
| **Verified resident** | Must be a verified member of the GN division (lives there, known locally). |
| **Platform capability** | Can use the platform independently (demonstrated during registration). |
| **No conflict of interest** | No political office, no competing rental business, no personal interests that could influence decisions. Signs a conflict-of-interest declaration. |
| **GN Officer endorsement** | Endorsement from the Grama Niladhari Officer or a recognised community organisation is preferred (the single highest-value check). |

**Public announcement and objection window:** the provisional appointment is posted publicly. The community is given 7 days to raise any serious, specific concern. If a resident raises a valid objection, the Admin reviews it before the appointment is finalised. Silence means confirmed.

**Conduct bond and trial:** the appointed moderator starts with the 500-point conduct bond active from day one. Their performance is evaluated after 3 months before confirming the appointment.

### **Phase 3 — Data-driven selection (ongoing)**

Once the platform has operating history, moderator selection becomes automatic. A member is eligible when they meet all of the following criteria:

| Eligibility criterion | Requirement |
| :---- | :---- |
| **Verified tenure** | Verified member for at least six months. |
| **Trust score** | Trust score of 70 or above (Section 6.3). |
| **Transaction history** | At least 10 successful completed transactions. |
| **Clean record** | No upheld claims and no suspensions. |
| **Residency** | Resident of the division. |
| **Platform capability** | Capable of using the platform independently. |
| **No conflict of interest** | No political office and no competing rental business. |

Members meeting every criterion form the eligible candidate pool. The Admin appoints one moderator per division from that pool, prioritising the highest trust score as a tie-breaker. The same 7-day public objection window applies.

## **17.2 Roles, responsibilities and authority** {#17.2-roles,-responsibilities-and-authority}

## **17.2 Roles, responsibilities and authority** {#17.2-roles,-responsibilities-and-authority-1}

* Verify new members (identity and address).

* Approve, adjust, or reject item listings.

* Review aid grant requests and vouch for genuine need.

* Facilitate dispute resolution and record the agreed outcome.

* Report disasters in their division to the Admin.

* Verify temporary-community membership requests.

## **17.3 Moderator compensation and recognition** {#17.3-moderator-compensation-and-recognition}

* A monthly stipend of 100 points from the Sponsor Pool, in recognition of their responsibilities.

* Recognition as a trusted community representative.

* Appointment itself reflects a high level of trust, a good transaction history, and a positive community reputation.

## **17.4 Limitations on moderator power** {#17.4-limitations-on-moderator-power}

**Rule 1 — Auto-escalation.** Whenever a Moderator is the borrower or lender on a booking, the platform automatically flags that booking. Any dispute bypasses the Moderator path entirely and goes straight to Admin.

**Rule 2 — Conduct bond.** Moderators hold a 500-point conduct bond. Penalties against a Moderator deduct from the bond first. If the bond runs to zero, moderation privileges are auto-suspended.

**Rule 3 — Sponsor Liaison oversight.** The Liaison receives a monthly summary of all transactions involving Moderators. Two or more unreturned items in any rolling 90-day period auto-flags to Admin.

**Rule 4 — Signed appointment acknowledgement.** At appointment, the Moderator signs an acknowledgement that misconduct results in removal, bond forfeiture, and a permanent entry in the division’s moderation history.

**The deepest protection is social:** a Moderator is someone the whole division knows by name and face. Stealing a neighbour’s drill costs them not just their bond and their role, but their standing in the community they live in.

# **18\. Account Lifecycle — Closure Types** {#18.-account-lifecycle-—-closure-types}

| Closure type | When it applies | What happens to the balance |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Type A — Standard** | Voluntary closure by any member. The default case. | Remaining points move to the Retired Pool and are recycled back to the Sponsor Pool on schedule. |
| **Type B — Donation (parting gift)** | Member leaving the area opts in to donate their balance on the closure screen. | Remaining points move to the GN division’s Aid Pool, tagged with the leaving member’s name as a parting gift. |

In all cases, the accounting invariant holds — points return to a pool inside the system. There is no cash outflow because there is no member cash inflow. Mithra is a closed-loop economy.

# **19\. Core Workflows (End-to-End)** {#19.-core-workflows-(end-to-end)}

## **19.1 Member registration** {#19.1-member-registration}

* User submits registration form: name, address, NIC, GN division, phone.

* Moderator verifies address and approves or rejects within 5 days.

* On approval, 200-point welcome bonus credited.

## **19.2 Listing an item** {#19.2-listing-an-item}

* Lender selects category and uploads photos.

* Enters proposed declared value; system shows proof requirements.

* Uploads proof or requests moderator inspection.

* Selects listing type: Rental or Donation.

* For rental: lender sets daily and/or monthly rate. Declared value shown for context.

* Moderator reviews and approves, adjusts, or rejects.

## **19.3 Borrowing** {#19.3-borrowing}

* Borrower picks item and duration; system highlights which rate option (daily or monthly) is cheaper.

* Borrower’s spendable balance must cover the rental \+ late-fee buffer. If insufficient, the system explains: list items to earn points, or request an Aid Grant.

* Request goes to lender; lender reviews borrower’s trust score and accepts or rejects within 24 hours.

* On acceptance, rental charge \+ late-fee buffer move from borrower wallet to In-Flight Pool.

* At pickup, both parties upload 1–5 condition photos. Both click Accept or Cancel.

* If both Accept: handover baseline locked. Rental charge to lender’s wallet. Borrower takes item.

* If either Cancels: booking cancels cleanly. All In-Flight points return to borrower.

* At return: both upload return-condition photos. Side-by-side comparison with handover baseline.

* If no damage: late-fee buffer returns (if on time), booking closes, both rate.

* If damage claim raised: moderator path per Section 10 — in-person resolution with three-party signoff.

## **19.4 Gifting** {#19.4-gifting}

* Sender opens recipient’s profile, enters amount and reason.

* System validates limits, balance, and absence of pending penalties.

* Points move immediately on confirm. Recipient notified.

## **19.5 Aid grant** {#19.5-aid-grant}

* Member submits request.

* Moderator vouches or rejects within 5 days.

* Liaison approves, adjusts, rejects, or asks for more info.

* Points move from Aid Pool to member wallet. 30-day use window; half of unused returns.

## **19.6 Disaster Mode** {#19.6-disaster-mode}

* Admin toggles for affected GN division with end date.

* All sponsors alerted to the emergency in that division.

* Platform connects interested sponsors directly with the moderator; relief arranged off-platform.

* Sponsor Liaison verifies and records any contribution for the sponsor’s CSR report. Admin switches the mode off when the emergency passes.

# **20\. Edge Cases** {#20.-edge-cases}

| Edge case | Handling |
| :---- | :---- |
| **Borrower doesn’t have enough points** | Platform explains: list items to earn points, or request an Aid Grant. No cash option. |
| **Simple-path cap reached; borrower accepts but lacks the points** | Auto-escalates to moderator path. Borrower’s partial balance is not touched until moderator agrees. |
| **Borrower accepts simple-path damage but later regrets it** | Acceptance is final after 48 hours but borrower can raise a separate dispute with Admin within 7 days. |
| **Moderator cannot meet within 7 days** | Admin reassigns to a moderator from a neighbouring division. The 7-day clock resets. |
| **Party refuses to sign off on moderator’s resolution** | Case escalates to Admin within 7 days. Admin’s decision is final; if still refused, account suspended. |
| **Borrower vanishes — no contact, no return** | After 14 days unreachable, total-loss admin escalation. Reserve Pool covers lender; account suspended. |
| **Moderator borrows an item and does not return it** | Auto-escalated to Admin (Rule 1). Penalty from conduct bond. Moderator suspended; replacement appointed. |
| **Item lost during rental (theft)** | Moderator path. Outcome depends on moderator’s decision — could be partial, none, or full penalty. |
| **Sender tries to gift while damage claim pending** | Blocked. UI explains: ‘Gifts are paused while you have a pending damage claim.’ |
| **Round-trip gifting pattern (A → B → A)** | Flagged to moderator after 3 round-trips in 30 days. |
| **Two members in same household request aid for same purpose** | Moderator detects via address; combines or rejects one. |
| **Power outage prevents app confirmation of handover** | 48-hour confirmation window; auto-cancel if no decision. |
| **Temporary membership expires mid-rental** | Active booking runs to completion. Only new bookings are blocked after expiry. |
| **Member applies for temporary community but proof is rejected** | Moderator rejects with a reason. Member can resubmit with better proof within 7 days before the request closes. |
| **Member tries to join two temporary communities simultaneously** | Blocked. Maximum of two communities total: one home, one temporary. |
| **Temporary membership expires, member forgets to extend** | 14-day grace window to resubmit. After 14 days, must re-apply fresh. |
| **Member promotes temporary to permanent but visits home community regularly** | After promotion, they can immediately apply to join old home as temporary (6-month window). Their old history in that community is visible. |

# **22\. System Architecture Overview** {#22.-system-architecture-overview}

Mithra is a server-rendered PHP application with a MySQL database. The front-end uses vanilla HTML, CSS, and JavaScript — no frameworks, no libraries. All page navigation is handled by the server; a thin AJAX layer provides polling-based updates for notifications and handover acceptance without full page reloads.

## **22.1 Architectural principles** {#22.1-architectural-principles}

* **Server-rendered pages.** PHP renders complete HTML for each navigation. Browser does a full page load on most actions.

* **Thin AJAX surface.** A small set of JSON endpoints (∼10–15) for actions that benefit from not reloading: polling for notifications, handover acceptance status, photo upload progress.

* **Session-based auth.** PHP $\_SESSION holds user ID and role after login. Custom RBAC middleware checks role on every request before the controller runs.

* **Append-only ledger.** Every point movement is an INSERT into point\_ledger inside a SERIALIZABLE transaction. Wallet balances are derived or cached and verified nightly.

* **Cron-driven scheduled work.** System cron runs PHP CLI scripts for stipends, late fees, festival drops, aid expiry, the nightly invariant check, disaster-mode end, and pattern detection.

* **Defence in depth.** PDO prepared statements, CSRF tokens on every form, htmlspecialchars() on all output, finfo MIME validation, GD re-encoding for uploads, password\_hash() with default cost.

## **22.3 Request lifecycle** {#22.3-request-lifecycle}

* **1\. Apache/Nginx receives request.** Rewrite rule sends everything to /public/index.php.

* **2\. Bootstrap.** Starts session, loads config, instantiates Router, registers middleware.

* **3\. Router matches URL.** Pattern → controller method. Attaches middleware chain.

* **4\. Middleware chain runs.** AuthMiddleware → RbacMiddleware → CsrfMiddleware → controller.

* **5\. Controller acts.** Calls services, queries via models, renders a view.

* **6\. View renders.** PHP includes partials for header, nav, page body, and footer. Output buffered and sent.

## **22.4 Key technical components** {#22.4-key-technical-components}

* **Item validation pipeline:** File upload via $\_FILES, finfo MIME check, GD re-encode, moderator review queue, audit trail.

* **Rate display engine:** Declared value \+ lender-set daily/monthly rates \+ requested duration → cheaper option highlighted for borrower.

* **Condition-photo pipeline:** Both-party upload, GD timestamp overlay, mutual acceptance, immutable baseline storage, CSS flexbox side-by-side comparison at return.

* **Damage state machine:** created → (simple or moderator path) → resolved → closed. Three-party signoff for moderator path.

* **Gifting rate-limiter:** Counter table updated per gift; pattern queries via SQL GROUP BY.

* **Notification system:** Notifications stored in DB; fetched via polling endpoint every 8 seconds using vanilla JS setInterval \+ fetch.

* **Scheduled jobs (cron):** Stipends, late fees, festival drops, aid expiry, nightly invariant check, disaster-mode end, suspicious-pattern detection, temporary membership expiry (auto-pause listings). Each logs to cron\_runs table.

* **Photo storage:** Local filesystem under /uploads/, organised by entity type. Photos served via PHP proxy script to allow access control.

# **26\. Feasibility Study** {#26.-feasibility-study}

This study assesses Mithra across five dimensions — technical, economic, legal, operational, and schedule — to confirm the project is realistic within a one-year academic timeframe and the stated constraints.

## **26.1 Technical feasibility** {#26.1-technical-feasibility}

* The stack is deliberately simple and well-understood: vanilla HTML/CSS/JS, pure PHP 8.x, MySQL 8.x (InnoDB) on a standard LAMP host — no frameworks to learn.

* All required capabilities use built-in PHP extensions (PDO, GD, finfo, session). No paid services or external APIs are needed.

* The four-member team has the skills for this stack; the constrained technology reduces rather than increases technical risk.

## **26.2 Economic feasibility** {#26.2-economic-feasibility}

* Zero member-facing cost — the platform is funded entirely by sponsors; members never pay.

* Hosting can run on a free or low-cost LAMP tier for the pilot; the only real cost is server hosting, drawn from the 10% sponsor operating-overhead bucket in production.

* No payment-gateway fees and no transaction costs, because no cash moves through the platform.

## **26.3 Legal feasibility** {#26.3-legal-feasibility}

Mithra is deliberately designed to avoid the legal status of a financial institution or fund:

* **No member money is held or moved.** 

* **Points are not currency —** 

they are a closed-platform credit with no cash-out path, so they are not e-money or a payment instrument.

* **Sponsor cash is handled offline** 

directly between the sponsor and the Sponsor Liaison; the platform only records the resulting points and never processes the cash itself.

* **Donations are item transfers, not money —** 

a donation moves ownership of a physical item between members with no money or points changing hands.

Remaining considerations — personal-data protection (member NIC and address, handled per Sri Lanka’s Personal Data Protection Act through access control and data minimisation) and a written sponsor agreement — are operational items to confirm before production. For the academic pilot, the no-member-cash design keeps the legal surface minimal.

## **26.4 Operational feasibility** {#26.4-operational-feasibility}

* The hyper-local design (one GN division) makes a real pilot achievable within a single community that already has a social fabric.

* The Moderator role provides on-the-ground governance without needing paid staff.

* Off-platform damage resolution and disaster relief rely on existing community relationships rather than complex automation.

## **26.5 Schedule feasibility** {#26.5-schedule-feasibility}

* A 12-month timeline with monthly milestones; 23 CRUD modules distributed across four members with a buffer over the 16-module minimum.

* The hardest month (booking, ledger, and condition photos) is de-risked by building reusable components in the preceding lighter month.
